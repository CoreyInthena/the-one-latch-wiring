Wiring for The One Latch control box in its current design. A 12 V LiFePO4 battery is charged by a small solar panel through a charge controller. A 5 A main fuse feeds the 12V+ hub, one of four labelled lever-nut hubs (12V+, GND, 5V, LGND). An LM2596 buck supplies 5 V to an Arduino Nano on a screw-terminal shield. Two relays reverse the 12 V latch actuator. The page also covers a Wiegand keypad, a switch that reads the latch's own door, and an I2C screen and clock. Build and safety notes

Power

A small solar panel, a charge controller and a 12 V LiFePO4 battery. On the latch side, the 5 A main fuse is the first thing on battery +. The charge controller's two battery leads also connect at the battery: BAT+ through its own fuse, and BAT−. Pulling the main fuse powers down the latch electronics (everything on the 12V+ hub): pull it before changing any latch wire, and keep 2-3 spare 5 A fuses in the box. The battery + run to the fuse holder (A1) also stays live with the fuse out; to work on A1 or A3, pull the battery spades first (they go back on with the fuse out, + first, then −). The main fuse does not isolate the charge controller: the controller's leads stay live on the battery, and the panel is live in daylight. To work on the solar wiring, take the panel off the controller first and the battery leads last.

Charge controller setting

Set the Wanderer's battery type to LITHIUM (LI) — before the panel is connected. Choose 12 V. The controller then asks for a charge voltage. Check before saving: the value is not settled on this page. Hold ENTER about 3 s to save, then open the setting again and check that it shows LI. The controller's lead-acid profiles are wrong for this LiFePO4 battery.

Charge controller order

Battery onto the controller's BAT terminals first, panel onto PV second. Disconnect in reverse: panel first, battery last. Re-check the battery-type setting whenever the battery has been off the controller.

LOAD terminals

The latch is powered from the battery through its own 5 A fuse. The charge controller's LOAD terminals are not used, so the controller does not cut the latch off at low battery.

Solar panel

Use a 12 V panel (about 18-21 V open circuit), never a 6 V panel. The current build uses a 5 W panel.

Polarity first

Before the buck sees anything, put the fuse in and meter the 12V+ hub against the GND hub; it must read positive. The LM2596 has no reverse-polarity protection.

Buck and Nano power

Set the buck to 5.00 V with nothing on its output before the Nano is ever connected. Feed the Nano's 5V screw, never VIN: 5 V into VIN runs the chip at about 4 V, out of spec. 12 V never touches the Nano shield.

Nano seating

Seat the Nano with its USB jack at the shield end marked with the USB outline. A Nano seated 180 degrees out dies at the first power-up. In the drawing the USB end is on the right (marked USB). The two rows are not lined up in the drawing, and the spacing is not to scale: on the shield the top-row GND faces the bottom-row 5V, so find each screw by its printed label.

Lever connectors

One wire per lever-connector slot, never two. A screw terminal may take two wires (the relay jumpers and the bottom-row GND screw do). Strip about 11 mm, twist the strands snug with no stray whiskers, put all copper inside the jaw, press the lever flat and tug-test. Never push a bare single 26-28 AWG jumper wire straight into a lever connector.

Wire gauges

18 AWG red/black for the 12 V current runs (battery, fuse, hubs to buck input, relay NO/NC feeds and jumpers). 22 AWG for logic and the A0 strap. 24 AWG pre-crimped female jumpers onto the header pins of the screen, clock and relay DC+/DC−/IN1/IN2.

Relays

Set both trigger jumpers (S1, S2) to LOW. A jumper on High holds that channel on at idle; a lit channel LED at idle is the tell. The relays are driven inverted on purpose: on a lock stroke relay 2 (IN2) pulls in and the black actuator lead rises to 12 V; on an unlock stroke relay 1 pulls in. Which relay clicks is not a fault; the only direction test is "unlock must retract".

Actuator safety

Keep hands clear of the rod whenever the fuse is in. Setup ends with a lock stroke, so set the unit up before the actuator leads (M5, M6) are landed. Once the box is set up, every power-up runs one 2.5 s lock stroke, and after an unlock the box can also run a lock stroke without a key press (for example when the latch door shuts; see the bench test note). Pull the fuse before swapping actuator leads.

USB safety

Take the 12 V off (pull the fuse) before plugging a USB cable into the Nano or unplugging the board. With the board unplugged, D2/D3 float and a low-trigger relay can switch on by itself.

Keypad

Only red, black, green and white are used; cap every other lead separately. The keypad's own back label outranks any colour table, and 10-lead units lack grey and brown. Arrival checks: red-to-black must not be a dead short, and green and white must idle between 3.0 and 5.5 V before they go near the Nano.

Screen

Use a 2.42 in board marked 3-5 V. Leave a service loop of at least 150 mm because the screen is in the control-box lid, and tug-test after closing it. A blank screen is usually just the screen timeout; the first key press only wakes it. If a key press does not wake it, check the battery. Check before wiring: some 2.42 in boards only answer on I2C after the pad marked D2 on the screen board itself (not the Nano's D2) is solder-bridged.

Latch-door switch

The switch reads the latch's own door, not the bin's lid. Nothing on the box senses the bin's lid, and the latch does not report lid openings. The screen's messages call this switch the lid (for example LID CLOSED); they mean the latch's own door.

A0 strap

The A0-to-GND strap is required. This build has no battery gauge, so the box gives no low-battery warning.

Manual release (not wired)

A manual release on the box unlocks the latch with no power and no code.

Bench test note

On the bench, after an unlock, open and then shut the latch door. About 0.6 s after the door presses the roller (D4 goes HIGH), the box drives a lock stroke. It does this only once the door has been seen open. With the roller released (latch door open), a relock from the keypad is refused. The screen calls this switch the lid (for example LID CLOSED); it means the latch's own door. This is a bench check, not a sales claim.
